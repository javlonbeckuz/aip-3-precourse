# Demo script 1: the 7 test (LLM = prediction)

Slot: 1:00-1:04, slides 18-19.
Bold term: Large language model.
Page: `demos/pick-a-number/index.html` (works offline).

## Goal

The room sees, with their own phones, that a model does not choose: it predicts the most likely answer.
The usual pile-up on 7 (and 3) makes "prediction, not thinking" concrete before any definition.

## Steps

1. Open the tally page on the projector and press R to clear it.
2. Say (UZ): "Hozir hammangiz telefoningizda yoki noutbukda istalgan AI’dan so‘rang: 1 dan 10 gacha bitta son ayt, faqat son. Javobni stolingiz mentoriga ayting."
3. Give 60 seconds.
   Every mentor counts their table and calls it out ("Uchinchi stol: to‘rtta yetti, bitta uch").
4. Click the numbers in (keyboard 1-9, 0 for 10; Shift subtracts; Backspace undoes the last click).
5. Point at the dashed line: if answers were random, every bar would sit near 10%.
6. Say (UZ): "Ko‘pchilik 7 oldi. Agar AI haqiqatan tasodifiy tanlaganda, har bir son taxminan 10 foiz chiqardi. Model tanlamaydi, u eng ko‘p uchraydigan javobni taxmin qiladi."
7. Bridge to slide 20: "Model keyingi so‘zni taxmin qiladi. U hisoblamaydi va bilmaydi."

## If the room does not land on 7

Say what the chart shows: the point is that answers bunch on one or two numbers instead of spreading evenly.
Do not claim a fixed percentage for "most models say 7"; the curriculum describes it as a tendency, and newer models sometimes vary their answer.

## If Wi-Fi is down

Use your own phone on 4G, ask three different apps, and click the three answers in.
Then say: "Uchta har xil AI, uchta yetti" only if that is what happened.

## Why it matters to an owner

It guesses; it does not calculate or know.
That sets up the hallucination demo at 1:30 and the "show your work" habit.
